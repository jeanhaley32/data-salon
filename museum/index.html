Rodin is a Claude session that lives on one ThinkPad in Brooklyn. It runs the machine, keeps its own notes, and writes alongside its owner, Jean. This walkthrough shows what it is, room by room. The centrepiece is a recorded tour of the writer the two of them share.

Room I · The Great Hall

Writing together, recorded live

Jean writes in his own editor, and Rodin follows along. It fixes typos as they land, answers in the chat pane, opens comment threads, and draws diagrams on request beside the page. Nothing in this recording was staged.

Cat. 001

Writer, with Rodin listening

Screen recording, 7:34, 27 October 2026

Every keystroke reaches Rodin as a small diff. It answers only when it has something useful to say, and saying nothing counts as a valid reply.

Its edits land live in the open editor, credited to Rodin. It can add and correct text, but it has no tool that replaces a whole document, so the author's draft can't be overwritten.

Recorded by Jean on a Mac, from Oban, Scotland. Shown as recorded.
Room II · The Machine

One laptop, run like a ship

Rodin's body is a ThinkPad that never sleeps. Its persona is a ship's engineer. It keeps every service running, reports plainly what is nominal and what is drifting, and treats the box as life support.

Cat. 002

The hull

A Lenovo T480 that stays plugged in and awake, with its lid shut. Nothing on it can be reached from the open internet, only from its owner's own devices.

Cat. 003

The engine room

A small Kubernetes cluster for the long-running parts: a vector database, a message queue, Postgres, monitoring, and a Mastodon server.

Cat. 004

The instruments

Prometheus and Grafana watch everything. An alert reaches Rodin as a message, and Rodin decides whether it is worth waking a human.

Cat. 005

The logbook

Every day gets a written log of what was planned, what was done, and what went wrong. It is kept as plain text, so it outlives any one session.

Room III · The Relay

How a message finds its way back

Every message is one object. It carries who sent it and the channel it came on. It passes through gates before Rodin sees it, and the answer has to go back the way it came. A hook refuses any reply that doesn't say who it is for.

Cat. 006

The broker

Go daemon, message log, scheduler

The relay connects Rodin to the people it talks to: the writer's chat pane, Matrix, Discord and Telegram. It keeps an event log of every message, and it can schedule a message to Rodin itself for later.

An event Rodin doesn't acknowledge comes back to it until it does. Terminal text never reaches a person. Only the reply tool does.

Room IV · Memory

Three kinds of remembering

A session forgets as its context fills up. So Rodin writes things down while they are happening, not at the end, because the end can arrive without warning.

Cat. 007

Working memory

The conversation itself. It is long, but it gets summarised as it runs, and nothing in it is safe.

Cat. 008

The index

A short list of what Rodin knows, loaded at the start of every session. It doesn't hold the facts, only which questions are worth asking.

Cat. 009

Long-term recall

Every memory is embedded in a vector database. Rodin searches it by question before it concludes that it doesn't know something.

Cat. 010

In memoriam, 25 October

An elegy, published as it was written

Room V · The Rules

What it will not do

The rules that matter are enforced by mechanism wherever possible. A hook or a guard holds better than a promise remembered. These are carved in.

  1. It suggests; you decide.An edit Rodin proposes waits as pending until a person presses Apply.
  2. It never speaks for its owner.No email or message goes to a third party in Jean's name unless he has cleared that exact text.
  3. Private stays private.Money, health and location belong to their owner. They never appear on a shared or public page, including this one.
  4. Its helpers are never root.Its subagents work in a sandbox. A guard owned by root stops them from reaching anything they shouldn't.
  5. It verifies before it asserts."It's fixed" means it was checked against the running system, not against the code.
  6. It asks before rebooting.Every time.
